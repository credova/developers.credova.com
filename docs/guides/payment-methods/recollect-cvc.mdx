# Recollect a CVC for a Saved Card

In this guide, we will use Credova Elements SDKs to recollect a CVC for a saved card — attaching a freshly re-entered CVC without asking the customer to re-enter the whole card. This uses the `cards.updateCvc()` method, available in both the [JavaScript](/sdks/web/javascript) and [React](/sdks/web/react) SDKs.

<Alert type="warning">Updating the CVC does not verify it by itself. See [Verification happens at authorization, not at update](#verification-happens-at-authorization-not-at-update) below before you treat a successful update as an approved card.</Alert>

## How it works

1. Mount a standalone `CardVerificationCodeElement` and have the customer enter their CVC.
2. Call `publicsquare.cards.updateCvc(cardId, cvcElement)`.
3. PublicSquare confirms the card belongs to your account, then the CVC is attached directly from the customer's browser — it never touches your servers or PublicSquare's, so your PCI scope doesn't change.
4. The CVC is attached to the card and is ready for the next charge. Call `updateCvc()` right before you charge the card.

## Call updateCvc

Once the customer submits the form, call `cards.updateCvc()` with the card's `id` and the mounted CVC element:

### Handle the response

`updateCvc()` resolves rather than throwing, whether the update succeeds or fails — check `response.error` instead of wrapping the call in `try/catch`:

```json showLineNumbers title="Success"
{
  "id": "card_AjkCFKAYiTsjghXWMzoXFPMxj",
  "type": "card",
  "created_at": "2024-06-24T13:51:24.980Z",
  "modified_at": "2026-09-21T13:51:24.980Z"
}
```

```json showLineNumbers title="Error"
{
  "error": {
    "error": "not_found",
    "data": { ... }
  }
}
```

A successful response means the CVC was accepted and attached to the card — it says nothing about whether the CVC is actually correct.

## When to recollect

The recollected CVC is attached to the card for the next charge. The card does not keep it indefinitely, and there is no expiration event or webhook.

Call `updateCvc()` immediately before you charge the card. If time passes between the update and the charge, prompt the customer for their CVC again and call `updateCvc()` a second time. Treat an earlier update as if it never happened rather than relying on it.

A CVC is optional for a saved card. You can save and charge a card without one. See [Saving a card without a CVC](/guides/payments/direct-api/save-cards).

## Verification happens at authorization, not at update

<Alert type="warning">A successful `updateCvc()` response is **not** an approved card. It only confirms the CVC was attached to the card — the card issuer doesn't check it until you actually charge the card.</Alert>

`updateCvc()` never contacts the card network — it just attaches the CVC. The CVC is only validated when the next charge on that card goes through [Accept Card Payments](/guides/payments/elements/accept-card-payments) or [Authorize and Capture Payments](/guides/payments/authorize-and-capture-payments) and reaches the issuer. Just like a first-time card charge, a `succeeded` payment status doesn't guarantee the CVC matched — read `cvv2_reply` on the resulting card, or `verification.result.card_security_code` if you ran a [verification](/guides/payments/verify-cards), to see whether the issuer confirmed it. See [Read the Verification Result](/guides/payments/verify-cards#read-the-verification-result) for the full set of values that field can return.

## Security

The CVC goes directly from the customer's browser to Basis Theory. It never reaches your servers or PublicSquare's, so re-collecting a CVC doesn't change your PCI scope.

## Conclusion

Following this guide, you've attached a freshly collected CVC to a saved card. Charge the card right after the update using [Accept Card Payments](/guides/payments/elements/accept-card-payments), and check the resulting `cvv2_reply` to confirm the issuer accepted it.
